# Licensing

Sqemes is **fair-code**, not open source. The source is public, you can read it, run it and change
it — and there is one thing you may not do with it, which is spelled out below.

We say "fair-code" rather than "open source" deliberately. Open source has a definition, our licence
does not meet it, and calling it open source anyway would be a small lie that costs more than it
gains.

## The one line that matters

**You may run Sqemes for yourself. You may not run it for someone else.**

Everything below is that sentence applied to real situations.

Those older releases have not been removed and will not be.

## "For yourself" — what that actually means

The test is **who the users are**, not who owns the server or whose hands are on the keyboard.

**You are running it for yourself** when the people signing in are your own — your employees, your
contractors, people working on your behalf. This includes using Sqemes to do work *for* your clients:
building assistants, writing skills, running an agency on top of it. That is your business, and it is
exactly what we hope you do with it.

**You are running it for someone else** when the people signing in are your client's people, or the
instance is presented as your client's, or your client is paying you for access to it. That needs a
commercial arrangement, and it is described in the next section.

### The cases, spelled out

| What you do | Allowed |
|---|---|
| Run an instance for your company, any size, commercially | ✅ |
| Use it to do client work — build their assistants, write their skills, hand over the results | ✅ |
| Run it privately, for yourself, for a side project | ✅ |
| Modify it for your own use, however heavily | ✅ |
| **You are an IT provider and you administer your client's own instance**, on their infrastructure, in their name | ✅ |
| **Run one instance and let your client's staff sign in to it** | ⛔ |
| Offer Sqemes as a hosted or managed service | ⛔ |
| White-label it, remove our notices, or resell it under another name | ⛔ |

**The fifth and sixth rows are the pair worth reading twice.** They can look identical from the
outside — in both, an agency is doing the operating. The difference is **whose instance it is.** If
the client holds the infrastructure and the instance is theirs, you are their administrator and that
is fine. If the instance is yours and you let their people in, you are providing the software to a
third party, and that is the case the licence covers.

## "So what do I do instead?"

**Your client takes a Sqemes Cloud contract, and you earn on it.**

There is deliberately no paid self-hosting licence. We are not going to sell permission to run
instances we cannot see, support or secure. If somebody's users need Sqemes, we would rather run it
ourselves and be accountable for it.

If you place Sqemes with clients regularly, talk to us — there is a partner arrangement, and the
early terms are better than the market. Write to support@sqemes.com.

Write to support@sqemes.com and describe what you want to do. **If you ask in good faith and we say
